# Additional clause checks

Clauses that are common in Israeli contracts, especially in tech, and need their own analysis
rather than a row in the main checklist. Each section ends with the grade to give and what to ask
for. Where the law is unsettled the section says so; do not present an unsettled point as a rule.

## 1. Non-solicitation of customers or employees

There is no statute and no binding ruling specific to non-solicitation that this skill relies on.
Treat it as a restriction on freedom of occupation and apply the same frame as a non-compete: a
restriction is generally void, and where an employer sues to enforce one, the employee's case is
that the restriction is unlawful or not proportionate and reasonable. A legitimate interest (a trade
secret, special consideration paid for the restriction, special funded training, a good-faith
duty) is what can carry it.

- **Grade:** Major for a ban on dealing with ALL of the employer's customers or recruiting ANY of
  its staff, or for a long period. Minor for a short ban limited to customers the employee
  personally handled and to active solicitation.
- **Ask for:** a limit to customers the employee personally handled in the last period of work, to
  active solicitation only (not accepting an approach), and a short period. State plainly that the
  enforceability of even the narrow version is unsettled.

## 2. On-call duty and rest-day work

Hours of Work and Rest Law 5711-1951:

- s.7(a): at least 36 consecutive hours a week are the employee's weekly rest.
- s.9: employing an employee during the weekly rest is prohibited unless permitted under s.12.
- s.17(a): work in the weekly rest is paid at no less than 1.5 times the ordinary wage, and the
  employer gives rest hours in its place as set in the permit. s.17(b) lets the employer of a
  monthly-paid employee give 1.5 hours of rest for each hour worked instead of the pay.
- s.9D1(a)(1): an employer may not refuse to hire someone only because they said they will not
  work on the weekly rest days, and may not demand an undertaking to work on them as a condition
  of hiring. This does not apply in the workplaces listed in s.9F(b) (public safety, state
  security or human safety and health, hotel hospitality, electricity production, essential
  services set by order) or in a workplace approved by the ministerial committee. s.9C gives the
  same protection on religious grounds.

The skill has no verified rule on how on-call availability itself must be paid. Do not invent one.

- **Grade:** Major for a hiring-condition undertaking to work rest days outside the s.9F(b)
  workplaces; Major for rest-day work with no premium stated; Major for open-ended 24/7
  availability with no pay, cap or rota.
- **Ask for:** removal of the rest-day undertaking, a stated 150% premium plus rest in lieu for any
  rest-day work, and an availability clause that states the hours, the pay and a cap.

## 3. Employee notice longer than the statute

The parties may agree a notice period longer than the statute. A longer contractual period binds
the employer: if it waives the employee's work during it, it pays for the whole contractual
period. Whether it also binds a resigning employee is split in regional case law. One line holds
that the longer period binds only the employer, so an employee who gives the statutory notice
cannot have the balance set off. The other, including סע"ש 28016-02-19, enforces the agreed
period against the employee too, provided it is mutual and reasonable. Without such an agreement
the employee cannot impose a longer period on the employer unilaterally.

- **Grade:** Minor when the longer period is mutual and moderate. Major when only the employee is
  bound, or when the contract lets the employer set off the balance or damages from final pay.
- **Ask for:** mutuality, and an express statement that no set-off from final pay applies beyond
  the statutory notice.

## 4. Leave forfeiture or no redemption

- Leave cannot be waived. With the employer's consent the employee may use part of the year's
  leave, at least 7 days a year, and carry the rest into the next two years. An employer's
  silence counts as consent to carry-over.
- To erase accrued days, the employer must prove it gave the employee the chance to take leave
  and the employee did not take it for reasons of their own.
- On termination the employee may redeem unused days for the final year and the 3 years before
  it.

- **Grade:** Major for "unused leave is forfeited at year end" without an offer-and-refusal
  mechanism, or for "no redemption on termination".
- **Ask for:** deletion, or a forfeiture rule that applies only after a written offer of dates the
  employee declined.

## 5. A manpower contractor as the employer

Check whether the contracting party is the workplace itself or a manpower contractor
(`kablan koach adam`) placing the employee there. Manpower-contractor workers carry their own set
of rights: a bar on employment through a manpower contractor beyond 9 months, equalisation of
terms with the end employer's own staff, a bar on charging the worker, and a bar on charging for
training. A training-cost clawback in a manpower contractor's contract is therefore a direct
breach, not only a deduction question. Private-sector and public-sector rules differ, so confirm
which applies.

- **Grade:** Major. The identity of the legal employer changes the whole review.
- **Ask for:** the name of the legal employer, the end-user workplace, and the planned placement
  period.

## 6. Pay secrecy

A clause forbidding the employee to tell anyone their salary has no statute behind it either way.
A regional labour court (Nazareth, סע"ש 57327-01-18, 12.08.2020) held that an employee may
disclose their own pay even under a contractual confidentiality duty, but that ruling is not a
binding precedent, and the national labour court has not ruled.

- **Grade:** Minor.
- **Ask for:** a carve-out for the employee's own pay. Say the point is unsettled.

## 7. A minor's contract (under 18)

- The minimum wage is a percentage of the adult figure (see `references/numerical-sanity-checks.md`).
- An employee under 18 is entitled to 18 days of annual leave regardless of seniority (Youth Labour Law s.27(a)). They are counted the way the Annual Leave Law counts days, weekly rest included, so on a 5-day week that is 14 working days.
- The written notice of terms is due within 7 days of starting, not 30.
- Ask the age before grading pay or leave, or the adult blockers will fire on a lawful contract.

## 8. Pregnancy protection during the current emergency period

A temporary provision of the Employment of Women Law, in force from 31.3.2026 to 31.3.2027
(s.9(c)(2c)), keeps certain unpaid-leave periods out of the count of the 60 protected days after
return from maternity leave. It is a post-hire point; mention it only if the contract tries to
define the protected period itself.

## 9. Privacy and monitoring

Tech contracts often carry a blanket consent to monitoring. Kol Zchut summarises the rules:

- An employer may not monitor an employee's personal correspondence in a personal or mixed
  mailbox without the employee's explicit, informed and freely given consent.
- Location tracking is allowed only for an essential purpose, proportionately, where there is no
  alternative, with full transparency and the employee's informed consent.
- Biometric attendance systems may be used proportionately and only with employees who
  expressly consent; offering a reasonable alternative is advised.
- Monitoring people who work remotely, including at home, is allowed only for a legitimate
  purpose, reasonably and proportionately, where there is no alternative.

- **Grade:** Major for a blanket, open-ended consent to monitoring of personal email, devices or
  location buried in the contract.
- **Ask for:** a monitoring clause limited to work systems and stated purposes, with no consent
  to reading personal correspondence, and an alternative to biometric attendance.
