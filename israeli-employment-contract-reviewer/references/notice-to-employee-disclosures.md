# Notice to Employee Law: Mandatory Written Disclosures

Source: Notice to Employee and Job Candidate (Employment Conditions and Screening/Hiring Procedures) Law, 5762-2002 (`חוק הודעה לעובד ולמועמד לעבודה (תנאי עבודה והליכי מיון וקבלה לעבודה), התשס"ב-2002`).

The employer must give the employee a written notice of employment terms within 30 days of the start of work (within 7 days for a minor under 18). A signed contract that already contains all the items below satisfies the duty. Use this list as a presence check during a contract review.

## The Mandatory List

When reviewing a contract, confirm each of these appears. A missing item is a finding (major if it is a pay or hours term, minor otherwise):

| # | Item | Notes |
|---|------|-------|
| 1 | Identity of the employer and the employee | Full legal names / company number |
| 2 | Start date, and the term if the contract is fixed-term | If it is not fixed-term, the notice must say so |
| 3 | The role | A summary of the main job |
| 4 | The direct supervisor | Name or job title |
| 5 | Total pay and the paydays | All wage components and when salary is paid (e.g., by the 9th of the month); where pay follows a collective-agreement grade, the grade and step |
| 6 | Basis of pay | Monthly salary, hourly, weekly, or output (productivity); and any part paid in kind, with its rate |
| 7 | Normal working hours | Length of the normal workday or workweek |
| 8 | The weekly rest day | Which day is the employee's weekly rest |
| 9 | Social-benefit contributions | Types of employer and employee payments and the bodies they are actually transferred to |
| 10 | Collective agreement | Where the employer is party to a collective agreement governing the role, the name and address of the workers' organisation that is party to it |
| 11 | Contractor terms | For an employee of a manpower or service contractor, the working terms in the contractor's contract with the end user, including terms on ending the employment |

The notice must also state that it derogates from no right the employee has under any law,
extension order, collective agreement or contract.

## How to Use This in Review

1. Walk the contract once against the table above, ticking each item.
2. For each missing item, add a finding: a missing pay component or hours term is a major finding (it is a statutory disclosure duty); a missing softer item (role summary, supervisor, workers' organisation details) is a minor finding.
3. If the contract is missing several items at once, recommend the employer reissue a complete written notice rather than patching clause by clause.

## Why it matters before signing

The disclosure duty has teeth, which is what gives the checklist leverage in negotiation:

- **Compensation without proof of damage.** Section 5(a)(1) lets the labour court award
  compensation even where no monetary damage was caused. For a knowing failure, section 5(b)
  allows exemplary compensation of up to 15,000 NIS, a figure indexed every 1 January, so quote
  it as the statutory base rather than as today's cap.
- **Burden of proof.** Under section 5A, where a term listed in section 2 is disputed and the
  employer did not give the required notice, the burden of proving that term moves to the
  employer, provided the employee testified to their claim.

So a missing written term is not only an omission. It is a term the employer may later have to
prove. The enforcement itself is a post-hire matter for `israeli-workplace-rights-navigator`.
