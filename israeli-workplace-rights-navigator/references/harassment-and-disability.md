## Workplace Harassment (Hatrada Minit)

Per the **Prevention of Sexual Harassment Law, 1998 (Chok LeMeni'at Hatrada Minit)** and the **Prevention of Sexual Harassment (Employer Duties) Regulations, 1998**. The regulations call the complaints officer the **acharai** (אחראי למניעת הטרדה מינית); in that text "memune" means a supervisor acting for the employer, so do not use it for the officer.

### What counts as sexual harassment (s.3(a))

| Clause | Conduct | Needs prior "not interested"? |
|--------|---------|-------------------------------|
| s.3(a)(1) | Extortion by threats where the demanded act is sexual | No |
| s.3(a)(2) | Indecent act | No |
| s.3(a)(3) | **Repeated** sexual propositions to a person who has shown they are not interested | Yes |
| s.3(a)(4) | Repeated references focused on the person's sexuality after they showed disinterest | Yes |
| s.3(a)(5) | A degrading or humiliating reference to the person's sex or sexuality, including sexual orientation | No |
| s.3(a)(5א) | Publishing, without consent, an image, film or recording focused on the person's sexuality, in circumstances where it may humiliate or degrade them (the statute lists defences for the publisher) | No |
| s.3(a)(6)(ג) | Propositions or references as in (3) or (4), so still **repeated**, to an employee within work relations, **by abuse of authority in work relations** | **No**: harassment even if the person never said no |

- **"Showed" disinterest** means by words or by conduct, provided there was no reasonable doubt about what the conduct meant (s.2). Unambiguous conduct counts: suddenly leaving the room, turning one's back, clear evasion (Kol Zchut).
- **Framework of work relations** covers the workplace, any other place of the employer's activity, work time, and anywhere authority in work relations is abused, including a supervisor's home (s.2, model takanon item 3).
- **Retaliation (hitnaklut, s.3(b))** is harm of any kind that originates in harassment or in a complaint or claim about it. It can target the victim, a witness, or anyone who supported the victim (Kol Zchut). s.4 prohibits both harassment and retaliation.

### Dating a coworker: how the law applies

Two layers, and answers must name both:
1. **The statutory test.** s.3(a)(3) and s.3(a)(6)(ג) both cover propositions "as in (3)", so both require **repetition**; abuse of authority removes only the need for a prior no. The bar is low: propositions repeated within minutes or within one event count as repeated (Kol Zchut).
2. **The case-law presumption** runs against the holder of authority, never against the junior party asking a senior one. Per Kol Zchut's summary of National Labor Court case law (ע"ע 274/06), in harassment by a holder of authority no expression of non-consent is required, and proving the elements shifts the burden to the holder of authority to rebut the presumption of abuse. Kol Zchut's guide accordingly says the law requires a manager to refrain **absolutely** from sexual propositions and references toward employees, even if they did not object.

| Situation | What to tell the user |
|-----------|----------------------|
| Peers, neither has authority or influence over the other | Asking out once is lawful under s.3(a)(3). After a no shown by words or by conduct that leaves no reasonable doubt (s.2), any further proposition is harassment. "Peers" does not cover single acts that are harassment on their own: an indecent act such as an unwanted kiss or touch (s.3(a)(2)) or a degrading remark (s.3(a)(5)). |
| Senior colleague with influence but no formal authority (another team, a project lead) | "Influence relations" lower the proof bar for showing disinterest compared with equal-status colleagues (Kol Zchut). Treat it as closer to the authority row than to the peers row. |
| A manager (direct, skip-level, or with influence over the person's status or future) asking out someone under them | **Do not.** Even where a single ask falls short of the statutory repetition test, the presumption of abuse of authority and the guide's "refrain absolutely" line put the risk on the manager, and repeated propositions are a criminal offence (s.5, 2 years). A subordinate's initiative does not by itself negate abuse of authority; courts weigh the power gap, the age gap, influence over status or future, and who initiated. |
| An existing, genuinely mutual relationship across a reporting line | Not unlawful in itself. Per Kol Zchut's summary of a Beersheba Regional Labor Court ruling (ס"ע 1319-07), a consensual senior-subordinate relationship does not contravene the law; the law intervenes where authority is abused. This protects a relationship that can be shown to be mutual, never the act of asking. It is a regional ruling and sits beside the presumption, so the senior party carries the risk. |
| The relationship started before one partner was promoted over the other | The authority relationship now exists, so the presumption above applies to the senior partner from that point. Reduce it: tell HR or the acharai, step out of decisions about the partner's pay, rating and promotion, and ask to change the reporting line. The law does not require these steps; they remove the facts the presumption rests on. |
| The relationship ended | The tests apply again: repeated propositions after a no, or by abuse of authority, are harassment, and harm at work that originates in harassment, or in a complaint or claim about it, is retaliation (s.3(b)). |

- **No statutory duty to disclose a relationship.** The 1998 law and its regulations, including the model takanon, contain no duty to report a workplace relationship. An employer may set one in its own policy, so check the takanon or employment terms.
- **Messages, WhatsApp and after hours.** Most s.3(a) definitions (clauses (1) to (5א)) are not limited to the workplace, while s.3(a)(6)(ג), the employer's s.7 duties and labor-court jurisdiction depend on the framework of work relations. Silence may not count as "showing" disinterest, so the practical step is to say no once in clear words in writing and keep screenshots. Sharing an intimate image falls under s.3(a)(5א).
- **When the employer learns of a manager-subordinate relationship.** The employer must take reasonable measures to prevent harassment and handle cases (s.7(a)); failing that duty makes it liable (s.7(c)). The law does not say which measure; with signs of harassment, the employer must refer the case to the acharai even without a complaint (reg.6(i)).
- **Harassment by a client or customer.** Labor-court exclusive jurisdiction covers harassment by the employer, a supervisor or a coworker (s.10(a)), so a claim against the customer personally falls outside it. (A business that uses agency staff is not a "customer" here: as a service orderer it carries employer duties.) The employee should still report it to the acharai.

### Employer obligations

- Every employer must take reasonable measures to prevent harassment and retaliation in work relations, and handle known cases effectively (s.7(a)).
- An employer of **more than 25** employees must set and publish a takanon (s.7(b)); failing to publish it is an offence punishable by a fine (s.8). An employer of 25 or fewer meets its duty by bringing the model takanon to every employee and supervisor (reg.2(b)). Workers of a manpower contractor employed at the site count as employees (reg.1).
- Every employer must let employees attend prevention training during working hours when a representative workers' organisation runs it, unless it runs such training itself (reg.2(d)).
- An employer that fails these duties is liable for harassment by its employee or supervisor within work relations (s.7(c)).
- **Acharai:** the employer appoints one (reg.4(a)), a woman where possible (reg.4(c)). The officer receives complaints, investigates and advises employees (reg.4(e)). An employer of fewer than ten employees may act as the officer itself (reg.4(h)).
- **Complaint handling:** a complaint may be made in writing or orally (reg.5(a)). The investigation must be efficient and without delay, with no fixed number of days (reg.6(d)); privacy and dignity are protected (reg.6(e)); the employer protects the complainant during the investigation and acts to separate the parties (reg.6(f)); the employer must refer a case to the officer even with no complaint or after a withdrawn one (reg.6(i)).
- **Decision:** after the officer's summary, the employer decides without delay and within no more than 7 working days (reg.7(a)), with written reasons to the complainant, the respondent and the officer (reg.7(b)).

**Service orderers.** Duties under this law reach beyond the direct employer to a business that engages workers through a contractor or manpower agency (mazminey sherut), so the site operator is not off the hook for agency staff. This matters for cleaning, security, catering and agency staff, who previously fell between the contractor and the site operator with neither taking responsibility.

### Complaint routes

The model takanon lists three routes, which may be combined (item 11). An internal complaint does not block the other two, and its outcome does not bind them (Kol Zchut).

| Route | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| Internal complaint to the acharai | Written or oral; process as above. |
| Police complaint | Harassment under s.3(a)(3), (4), (5) and (6) is an offence carrying 2 years' imprisonment, retaliation 3 years, and both together 4 years (s.5). A criminal limitation period applies; do not delay. |
| Civil claim | Against the employer, a supervisor or a coworker: the regional labor court, which has exclusive jurisdiction (s.10(a)); against a customer personally: outside the labor court's exclusive jurisdiction (s.10(a)). In an employee's claim under s.7 of the Equal Employment Opportunities Law, once the worker proves one of the conditions in that section, the burden shifts to the employer, provided 3 years have not passed since the cause arose (s.6א). It can name the harasser and the employer. Limitation: 7 years (s.6(c) with Limitation Law s.5). The court may award compensation without proof of damage of up to NIS 120,000 per the statute text, CPI-linked each 1 January (s.6(b)). |
| Equal Employment Opportunities Commission | Handles workers' complaints against employers under the employment-equality laws, and in suitable cases sues on the worker's behalf in the labor court (Kol Zchut). |

## Disability Accommodations

Per the **Equal Rights for People with Disabilities Law, 1998 (Chok Shivyon Zchuyot Le'anashim Im Mugbaluyot)**:

**Employer obligations:**
- Provide reasonable accommodations (hat'amot) for employees with disabilities
- Cannot discriminate in hiring, promotion, or termination based on disability
- Must ensure physical accessibility of the workplace

**Reasonable accommodations include:**
- Modified work hours or schedules
- Accessible workstation equipment
- Work-from-home arrangements when feasible
- Modified job duties that do not fundamentally change the role
- Additional break time as needed

**Key rules:**
- Applies to employers with 25+ employees (some provisions apply to smaller employers)
- The employer is not required to make accommodations that impose an "undue burden" (natel bilti savir)
- The employee should notify the employer of the need for accommodations
- Disputes can be brought before the Equal Employment Opportunities Commission or labor court
