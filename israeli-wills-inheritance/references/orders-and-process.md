# Inheritance Orders & the Registrar Process

After a death, the estate is released only once the right order is issued by the Inheritance Registrar (הרשם לענייני ירושה). Picking the wrong order is the most common time-waster.

## Which order do I need?

| Did the deceased leave a will? | Order | Hebrew | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| No will | Succession order | צו ירושה | Declares the legal heirs and their shares by law. |
| Yes, there is a will | Probate / will-execution order | צו קיום צוואה | Gives the will binding legal effect and distributes per its provisions. |

Both are filed with the **Inheritance Registrar**, online at the Registrar portal (inheritance.justice.gov.il). The Registrar issues most orders.

A will is inert until its order issues: under Section 39 no right can be claimed under a will, and it cannot be relied on as a will, unless a probate order has been granted.

## Which forum: Registrar, Family Court, or religious court

The default is the Registrar. Section 67א lists the ONLY grounds on which a pending application is transferred to the **Family Court**:
1. an objection to the application was filed;
2. the State or one of its institutions is a party to the application;
3. the Attorney General or their representative saw fit to open a proceeding on the application or to join one;
4. the Public Trustee (האפוטרופוס הכללי) represents a person whose property it manages;
5. the Registrar saw fit to transfer the application to the court.

Grounds 3 and 4 are the ones that bite in ordinary family files: an estate with a minor heir or an heir under guardianship commonly ends up in court through them. "The will looks defective" is NOT a transfer ground.

A **religious court** (rabbinical, sharia, druze) is a third forum. Under Section 155 it may issue a succession order or a probate order, but only where every party concerned has expressed written consent.

If a fee was already paid to the Registrar and the matter then moves to court under Section 67א, that payment counts as a fee paid to the court (regulation 6 of the fee regulations). The applicant does not pay twice.

## The online application steps
1. Open an application on the Registrar's online portal.
2. Identify the deceased and (for a succession order) the legal heirs, or (for a probate order) submit the ORIGINAL will.
3. Pay the fees (see the schedule below).
4. The Registrar publishes a notice and sets a period for objections, which by law cannot be shorter than two weeks and in practice is 14 days. An objection (התנגדות) can be filed within that period as long as the order has not yet been issued.
5. After the objection window the order is issued. Orders are now issued digitally only; the Registrar does not print orders or hand out copies at its offices. The digital order is transmitted to banks, the Land Registry (Tabu), insurers, and other bodies, so heirs can act on it.

Three rules that sit on top of the table:
- **Online filing costs 85% of the listed fee.** This is why an online succession-order application is quoted at 507 NIS: 597 reduced to 85%. A manual filing pays the full 597. A represented applicant must file online.
- **A separate publication fee of 66 NIS** applies to a succession-order or probate-order application.
- **The amounts change on 1 January each year** by the CPI movement published the preceding November, rounded to the nearest whole shekel.

Fee exemptions (regulation 4): the State and anyone authorised to represent it, the Public Trustee, the National Insurance Institute, a local-authority welfare officer acting in that capacity, and an applicant for a succession order who is the spouse, child, father, mother, or sibling of someone who died as a result of war operations (after 29 November 1947), as a casualty of a hostile act, or in military service.

## Legal heirs when there is NO will (Section 11)
The surviving spouse takes the household chattels including the family car. Of the REST of the estate:
- with children (or their descendants) OR with the deceased's parents: the spouse takes **one-half**;
- with the deceased's siblings (or their descendants) or grandparents: the spouse takes **two-thirds**;
- if none of those relatives survive: the spouse takes the **whole** estate.
The remaining share goes to the first surviving class: children split it equally (a deceased child's share passes to that child's own children); if there are no descendants, the parents and their line; then grandparents and their line.

This is the core of Section 11. For the exact split in a specific family, the succession-order application asks for the family structure and the Registrar applies the law.

**The apartment proviso.** The two-thirds branch carries a condition that is easy to miss and often decides the outcome: if on the eve of the death the spouse had been married to the deceased for three years or more and was living with them at that time in an apartment wholly or partly included in the estate, the spouse takes the deceased's ENTIRE share in that apartment, and two-thirds of what remains of the rest of the estate.

**Marital property comes first.** Section 11(c) deducts from the spouse's share anything due to them on a ground arising from the marriage, including a כתובה, but expressly excludes what is due under חוק יחסי ממון or a property agreement. That marital-property entitlement is ranked as an estate debt under Section 104(a)(4), which means it is settled BEFORE the estate is divided among heirs. Describing the spouse's position as "half" without this understates it.

**Unmarried partners (Section 55).** Where a man and a woman lived a family life in a shared household without being married to each other, and at the time of death neither was married to anyone else, the survivor is treated as though the deceased had left them by will whatever they would have taken by law had the couple been married. This yields to any contrary provision, express or implied, in a will the deceased actually left.

## Disclaiming a share (Section 6)

An heir who does not want the share, typically because the estate may be insolvent, files a written disclaimer (הסתלקות) with the Inheritance Registrar, or with the court once the file has moved there under Section 67א. The constraints:
- It is available only after the death and only while the estate has not yet been distributed.
- It may cover the whole share or part of it, or a whole or partial legacy under a will.
- The disclaimer is retroactive: the heir is treated as never having been an heir to that extent.
- It CANNOT be directed to a person of the heir's choosing. A disclaimer in favour of another person is permitted only where that person is the deceased's spouse, child, or sibling.
- A conditional disclaimer is void.
- A minor or a person declared legally incompetent needs court approval to disclaim.
- Section 7(c): an heir who has already transferred or charged their share in the estate has lost the right to disclaim it.
- Section 6א (added 2024) widens the permitted beneficiaries of a disclaimer where the deceased died in the war or in a hostile act, allowing it in favour of family members listed in Section 10 and of the deceased's former spouse or child.

## Tax on an inheritance

There is no estate tax and no inheritance tax in Israel. The Estate Tax Law 1949 was repealed for the estate of anyone who died after 31 March 1981, and from 1 January 1988 the repeal was extended to estates of those who died before that date. Receiving an inheritance, whether cash, securities, or real estate, is not itself a taxable event.

The exposure arrives later, when an inherited asset is SOLD: מס שבח (land appreciation tax) on real estate, or capital gains tax on other assets, can apply, and the reliefs available to an heir are their own subject. Route that question to a tax professional; it is outside this skill.

## Two limits and one reassurance
- **Maintenance from the estate (Section 56):** a spouse, children, or parents who genuinely need support are entitled to maintenance from the estate, whether it passes by law or by will. A will cannot simply cut off a needy dependent.
- **No forced heirship:** otherwise a testator is free to leave their property to whomever they choose; Israel has no fixed "reserved portion" beyond the maintenance right above.
- **Debts:** heirs receive what remains after the estate's debts. If the debts may exceed the assets, get advice before accepting, an heir can disclaim their share (הסתלקות) instead of taking on a negative estate.
- **Outside the estate entirely (Section 147):** amounts payable on death under an insurance contract, a pension fund, or a provident fund are not part of the estate unless the contract stipulates that they go to the estate. They pass to the beneficiaries (מוטבים) named at the institution, and neither the will nor the succession order redirects them.

## When to send the user to a lawyer
- An heir disputes the will or the heirs (an objection is likely).
- The estate has a business, foreign assets, a trust, or debts that may exceed assets.
- Minor children need a guardian appointed.
- The will's validity is in doubt (form defects, capacity questions, undue influence under Section 35).
