# Rules detail and letter building blocks

All section numbers refer to the Consumer Protection Law 1981 (CPL), the Consumer Protection (Cancellation of Transaction) Regulations 2010 (Cancellation Regs) and the Consumer Protection (Warranty and Service After Sale) Regulations 2006 (Warranty Regs). Sources and verbatim quotes are in `../evidence.json`.

## 1. When there is NO cancellation right

Check these before demanding a no-reason refund. A cancellation because of a defect, non-conformity or late delivery is a different claim and is not blocked by this list.

**Distance sales (s.14C(d))**
- Perishable goods.
- Lodging, travel, vacation or leisure services, if the cancellation falls within seven non-rest days before the service.
- Information as defined in the Computers Law.
- Goods made specially for the consumer.
- Goods that can be recorded, copied or duplicated, once the original packaging was opened.

**In-store (Cancellation Regs reg.6), selected items**
- Food products.
- Furniture assembled in the consumer's home, goods made to measure, medicines and supplements, perishables.
- Underwear and swimwear.
- Jewelry above NIS 3,000 (watches are not excluded).
- Gift cards and vouchers used as payment.

**Who is NOT protected at all:** deals between two private people and business-to-business deals.

## 2. In-store periods (Cancellation Regs reg.2)

| Goods | Period |
|---|---|
| Furniture, home and garden equipment, electrical and electronic devices, unopened goods, goods ordered and not supplied, water purifiers, watches (above NIS 50) | 14 days from receipt |
| Clothing and footwear (price tag not removed) | Two non-rest days after purchase (reg.2(2)) |
| Jewelry up to NIS 3,000 | Two non-rest days after purchase (reg.2(7)) |
| Schedule services: cosmetics, gyms, dating clubs, telecom and internet providers and more | 14 days from the deal or written contract, whichever later, even if the service started (reg.2(4)) |
| Goods or services sold at a sales event the dealer invited people to | Goods: 14 days from receipt; a one-off service: within 14 days and at least two non-rest days before it starts (reg.2(5)) |

Conditions: goods returned undamaged and unused (connecting to electricity, gas or water counts as use), with an invoice, till slip or exchange note (reg.3(a)). A franchisee with a posted notice may refuse refunds for goods bought at another branch (reg.3(e)). Goods not on the schedule have no statutory in-store right; only the shop's posted policy (s.4C) applies.

Refund: at the time of cancellation if possible, and no later than seven business days, by the same payment method (reg.4(a)). Fee: 5% or NIS 100, whichever is lower (reg.5(a)).

## 3. Letter structure (all tracks)

1. Header: date, recipient (company name, customer-service email or address), sender name, ID number, phone, customer or order number.
2. Subject line naming the claim type.
3. Facts in short dated lines.
4. The legal basis, one or two sections, quoted briefly.
5. One demand: what, how much, by when.
6. Escalation sentence.
7. Signature placeholder.

> [בעסקה באשראי או בתשלומים: לפי סעיף 6א לחוק, לעסקה אין תוקף כל עוד לא הסכמתי לה, ואיני מסכים/ה.]

**Escalation sentence (all tracks)**
> אם לא אקבל מענה עד [תאריך], אפנה למועצה הישראלית לצרכנות ולרשות להגנת הצרכן ולסחר הוגן, ואשקול הגשת תביעה לבית המשפט לתביעות קטנות.

Add an exemplary-damages sentence ONLY for a breach on the s.31A list:
> מכתב זה מהווה גם דרישה בכתב כאמור בסעיף 31א(ב) לחוק.

For a telecom company, add: "ואפנה גם לפניות הציבור במשרד התקשורת" and attach this letter and the company's answer to the Ministry complaint.

## 5. Complaint to the Authority (Track D)

Describe: who the consumer is (age or status), how the contact started (cold call, door knock), what was said, the price, what was charged, whether a disclosure document was given, and whether the consumer understood the language. Attach the cancellation letter and any recording or SMS. Explain to the user that the Authority uses the complaint for enforcement and will not send them a refund.

## 6. Track C and Track D detail

**Warranty objections and the answer**

- **"We are only a parallel importer":** an importer counts as the manufacturer for the goods it imported (reg.18), parallel or official; no parallel-import exception appears in the regulations.
- **"Go to the importer, not us" (from the shop):** for the warranty duties the shop is right, so address the letter to the importer named on the warranty certificate (ask the user which one). The shop carries those duties only when the manufacturer cannot be located (reg.19, goods above NIS 400), and it stays answerable as the seller under the Sale Law (next block).
- **"No warranty certificate, no service":** a valid original warranty sticker on the goods is enough; the dealer may not demand the certificate as well (CPL s.18A(a1)(2)). A buyer who received the goods from a previous owner inherits the warranty rights (reg.17).
- **Extended warranty bought with the goods:** a paid repair plan for after the warranty (a "long-term service") sold together with the goods can be withdrawn from until the end of the warranty period set for the goods (reg.16(b)).

**Goods outside the Warranty Regulations** (furniture without a mechanism, clothing, goods of NIS 150 or less, or a defect after the warranty), and as a claim against the SELLER alongside them: the route is the Sale Law 1968.

- The buyer must inspect the goods immediately on receipt (s.13) and notify the seller immediately after that inspection or after discovering the defect, WHICHEVER IS EARLIER (s.14(a)). Late notice loses the right to rely on the defect (s.14(b)). So ask when the defect could first have been seen.
- Only a defect a reasonable inspection could not reveal runs from discovery. Notice must still be immediate, and the buyer cannot rescind after two years from delivery or claim other remedies after four (s.15). These limits lift if the seller knew or should have known and did not disclose, but notice on discovery is still required (s.16).
- The notice describes the defect in reasonable detail and lets the seller inspect (s.17).
- Default demand: repair or replace within a set period, adding that otherwise the buyer will cancel and ask for a refund. For a breach that is not fundamental, cancellation requires first giving the seller an extension (Contracts (Remedies) Law s.7(b)). The letter does not decide whether the breach is fundamental, and what a court would award is not computed here.

**Exploitation and deception (CPL s.32)**

- After those 4 months, or for anyone else: s.32 lets the consumer cancel a deal involving deception (s.2) or unfair influence (s.3), if it was MATERIAL in the circumstances. Unfair influence includes exploiting a mental, cognitive or physical limitation or the consumer's insufficient grasp of the language of the deal (s.3(b)(5)-(6)), preventing the consumer from consulting, repeated home visits or calls after being asked to stop, threats, and supplying a paid item that was not explicitly requested (s.3(b)(2)-(4), (7)-(8)). s.32 still refers to "exploiting distress" in s.3, the section's old wording; applying it to every s.3 limb follows the cross-reference, but no ruling was checked.
- The consumer may cancel within a **reasonable time** from when they learned of the deception, or learned that the exploitation had ended. The dealer refunds within seven days, the same way the consumer paid; the consumer makes the goods available where they were delivered, and pays the proportional price of a continuing service already given (s.32(a)-(b)). This is not a fixed deadline, so tell the user to act quickly and describe the facts precisely.
